Rules this outline already obeys:

- **Earth sciences, plus a planetary and astronomy strand.** Geology,
  geophysics, geochemistry, the atmosphere, the ocean, the cryosphere,
  climate and physical geography make up about four fifths of the series.
  The rest is planetary science, spherical astronomy, star and planet
  formation, the Milky Way and galaxies, and the origin of the elements
  (five astronomy chapters at university, Y1–Y3).
- **Overlaps: recall, then build.** Where *One Physics Book*, *One Biology
  Book* or *One Chemistry Book* already owns a notion (the cross-series table
  at the end), that series stays the owner. A chapter here opens with a
  `recall` box that names it in prose ("as the physics book shows"), never
  with a `\cref`, and only takes the Earth or space angle from there. Where
  the other series' owner comes at a *later* grade, this series introduces
  only what it needs, from its own angle (fossils here are clocks and
  environment markers; in biology they are evidence of evolution).
- **Books 1 and 2 never repeat themselves.** Every notion has one owner
  chapter, the earliest grade where it fits. A later chapter that needs it
  opens with a 3–5 line *You already know* recall box and adds only what is
  new (ownership map at the end). The university books may re-found a school
  notion rigorously, but never re-teach it at the school level, and no notion
  is owned by two university books.
- **Climate change: physical science only.** Radiative forcing, the
  observed records, attribution and projections are taught as the scientific
  assessments state them (IPCC Working Group I), with their confidence
  levels, and from sourced data. No policy recommendation anywhere: policy
  and costs belong to the economics series.
- **Geological maps** are drawn exactly in TikZ, from one invented but
  geologically consistent region that recurs through the series. Real maps
  appear only when they are public domain (USGS, NASA, NOAA), credited per
  file. Real places are named freely (the Alps, Hawaii, the Himalaya).
- **Observation only.** The books may invite the reader to observe the sky,
  the weather, a landscape, building stones or a garden soil. They never
  propose hammering, quarries, cliffs, caves, streams in flood, or looking at
  the Sun. Professional fieldwork and lab work are described in *In the field*
  and *In the lab* boxes. Each university book has one field-and-lab methods
  chapter.
- **Physical geography** (maps and orientation, the physical world map,
  climate zones, landforms) lives in Books 1–2. Human geography belongs to
  the country books and economics.
- Printed text never names a curriculum, a country's school system or a
  university. Provenance lives in `sources/outline_sources.md` and in source
  comments only.

## Book 1 — Grades 1–9

### Grade 1
1. **Watching the Weather** — `watching-the-weather`
   Sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy, snowy; warm and cold; a weather chart kept
   for a week; dressing for the weather. (Day and night are recalled from the
   physics book.)
2. **Landscapes Around Us** — `landscapes-around-us`
   Mountains, hills, valleys, plains, rivers, lakes and the coast; natural
   and built parts of a landscape; describing and drawing one.

### Grade 2
1. **Water on Earth** — `water-on-earth`
   Oceans and seas (salt water), rivers, lakes, ice and snow (fresh water);
   most of the Earth's surface is covered by water; where rain goes.
2. **From a Drawing to a Map** — `drawing-to-map`
   A view from above; the plan of a room and of a street; legend and
   symbols; the four cardinal directions and the compass rose.

### Grade 3
1. **Stones, Pebbles and Sand** — `stones-pebbles-sand`
   Rock is the solid ground under the soil; pebbles rounded by rivers and
   waves; sand is broken rock; hard and soft rocks; the stones we build with.
2. **The Globe: Continents and Oceans** — `continents-and-oceans`
   The globe as a model of the Earth; the equator, the poles and the two
   hemispheres; seven continents and five oceans; a world map is a flattened
   globe.

### Grade 4
1. **Clouds, Rain and Snow** — `clouds-rain-snow`
   Clouds are droplets or ice crystals; the main cloud families (heaped,
   layered, wispy); rain, snow and hail; measuring rainfall and temperature;
   a class weather station.
2. **Volcanoes and Earthquakes: A First Look** — `volcanoes-earthquakes-first-look`
   What an eruption looks like (lava, ash, gas); what an earthquake does;
   they happen in some regions much more than in others; staying safe.
3. **The Physical Map of the World** — `world-physical-map`
   Reading a physical map (colours for altitude and depth); the great
   mountain ranges, rivers, lakes and deserts; the highest, longest and
   largest, with scale comparisons.

### Grade 5
1. **Water Shapes the Land** — `water-shapes-the-land`
   Weathering breaks rock, erosion carries it away, deposition lays it down;
   a river from its source to its mouth; meanders and deltas; floods.
2. **Weather and Climate** — `weather-and-climate`
   Weather is today, climate is the long-run average; a climate chart
   (temperature curve and rainfall bars); hot, temperate and cold zones;
   deserts, rainforests and polar regions.
3. **Natural Hazards** — `natural-hazards`
   Storms, tropical cyclones, floods, droughts, earthquakes and eruptions as
   natural hazards; a hazard becomes a disaster where people live; warnings
   and safety rules.

### Grade 6
1. **The Earth, a Planet of the Solar System** — `earth-a-planet`
   Recalls the Solar System from the physics book, then: rocky and giant
   planets; Earth, Venus and Mars compared (temperature, atmosphere, water);
   what makes life possible on the Earth.
2. **Latitude, Longitude and Time Zones** — `latitude-longitude`
   Parallels and meridians; locating any place by two angles; the prime
   meridian and the date line; time zones from the Earth's rotation.
3. **Rocks and Minerals** — `rocks-and-minerals`
   A rock is made of minerals; crystals; the three rock families by how they
   form (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic) and the rock cycle that links
   them; recognising common rocks (granite, basalt, limestone, sandstone,
   clay, marble, slate); scratch hardness.
4. **Inside the Earth** — `inside-the-earth`
   Crust, mantle and core; how deep we have drilled and how earthquake waves
   tell us the rest (told); temperature rises with depth.
5. **Water Underground** — `water-underground`
   Infiltration; porous and permeable rocks; the water table; springs and
   wells; aquifers (the water cycle is recalled from the physics book).

### Grade 7
1. **Plate Tectonics: The Moving Puzzle** — `plate-tectonics`
   Wegener's evidence (fitting coastlines, fossils, rocks, ancient climates);
   lithospheric plates; divergent, convergent and transform boundaries;
   speeds of a few centimetres per year, measured by satellite positioning.
2. **Volcanoes** — `volcanoes`
   Magma, magma chamber, vent and crater; effusive and explosive eruptions
   and why (viscosity and gas); volcanic products; where volcanoes are
   (ridges, subduction zones, hotspots).
3. **Earthquakes** — `earthquakes`
   Faults, focus and epicentre; seismic waves (told); the seismometer;
   magnitude and intensity; earthquakes along plate boundaries; tsunamis.
4. **Sediments and Fossils** — `sediments-and-fossils`
   Sediments settle in layers and harden into rock; how a fossil forms and
   why most living things leave none; layers and fossils as a record.
5. **The Ages of the Earth** — `geological-time`
   Relative dating (superposition, cross-cutting, inclusion, fossil
   succession); eras and periods; 4.6 billion years as a calendar year;
   absolute ages exist (the method is in the physics book).

### Grade 8
1. **The Atmosphere** — `the-atmosphere`
   Layers of the atmosphere and how temperature changes with height;
   pressure falls with altitude; the ozone layer (told); composition
   recalled from the chemistry book.
2. **Winds and the Global Circulation** — `winds-global-circulation`
   Uneven heating with latitude; rising and sinking air; high and low
   pressure (recalled from the physics book); sea breezes; trade winds and
   westerlies; the Earth's rotation deflects winds (told). Explains the
   climate zones.
3. **Air Masses, Fronts and Weather Maps** — `fronts-and-weather-maps`
   Air masses; warm and cold fronts; depressions and anticyclones; isobars;
   reading a weather map; how a forecast is made.
4. **The Oceans** — `the-oceans`
   Ocean basins and the seafloor (shelf, slope, abyssal plain, ridge,
   trench); salinity; surface currents driven by winds (gyres, the Gulf
   Stream); deep currents of cold, salty water (told); the ocean carries heat.
5. **Ice on Earth** — `ice-on-earth`
   Glaciers, ice sheets, sea ice and permafrost; how a glacier flows and
   erodes (U-shaped valleys, moraines); the ice ages (told).

### Grade 9
1. **The Earth's Climate, Past and Present** — `climate-past-and-present`
   The greenhouse effect (recalled from the chemistry book) and the Earth's
   temperature; past climates (ice ages, warm periods); today's records
   (temperature, carbon dioxide, sea level, ice); the causes as the
   scientific assessments state them.
2. **Living with Natural Risks** — `natural-risks`
   Risk = hazard × vulnerability (qualitatively); forecasting versus
   prevention; monitoring volcanoes and faults; flood zones; earthquake-safe
   building (principles only).
3. **Resources from the Earth** — `earth-resources`
   Recalls resources from the chemistry book, then their geology: how ore
   deposits form; coal, oil and gas from buried organic matter; groundwater;
   sand and aggregates; renewable and finite on human and geological time.
4. **Landforms** — `landforms`
   Relief as a balance between uplift and erosion; landforms made by wind
   (dunes), the sea (cliffs, beaches, spits) and dissolution (karst, caves);
   reading a landscape.
5. **Maps of the Whole Earth** — `maps-of-the-earth`
   Why a flat map must distort (Mercator and equal-area projections); scale;
   satellite images of the Earth; satellite positioning (principle).

## Book 2 — Grades 10–12

### Grade 10
1. **Measuring the Earth** — `measuring-the-earth`
   Evidence for a round Earth; Eratosthenes; measuring a meridian by
   triangulation; the Earth's radius and flattening; geodesy today.
2. **A Habitable Planet** — `habitable-planet`
   Distance to the star sets the temperature range; mass holds an
   atmosphere; liquid water; the habitable zone; planets of other stars
   (first look).
3. **The Earth's Energy Budget** — `earth-energy-budget`
   Sunlight received by latitude and season; albedo; the global energy
   budget in watts per square metre; less than 1 % goes into
   photosynthesis; the rest drives winds, currents and the water cycle.
   (The quantitative greenhouse model belongs to the physics book.)
4. **Why Clouds Form** — `why-clouds-form`
   Humidity, saturation and dew point; rising air cools; stable and
   unstable air; how droplets grow into rain and snow.
5. **Storms and Tropical Cyclones** — `storms-and-cyclones`
   Thunderstorms and lightning; tornadoes; the anatomy and energy source of
   a tropical cyclone; intensity scales; storm surges.
6. **The Layered Ocean** — `layered-ocean`
   Temperature and salinity with depth; the mixed layer, thermocline and
   deep ocean; seawater density; upwelling and where the ocean is
   productive.
7. **Soil, a Slow-Forming Resource** — `soil-formation`
   Recalls soil from the biology book, then the geological side: weathering
   of the bedrock, hydrolysis, clay and humus; how slowly soil forms and how
   fast it can be lost; soils and climate.
8. **Drainage Basins, Rivers and Floods** — `drainage-basins`
   The drainage basin; river discharge and the hydrograph; floods and their
   return period; dams and levees as physical structures.
9. **Freshwater Resources** — `freshwater-resources`
   Stocks and flows of fresh water; residence times; aquifers as reservoirs;
   uneven distribution; overuse and depletion (the Aral Sea, falling water
   tables) as physical facts.

### Grade 11
1. **From Continental Drift to Seafloor Spreading** — `drift-to-spreading`
   Why drift was rejected; mapping the ridges; seafloor spreading; magnetic
   reversals and the striped seafloor (geomagnetism recalled from the
   physics book); ages of seafloor sediments.
2. **Seismic Waves Reveal the Interior** — `seismic-structure`
   P and S waves and their travel times; shadow zones; the Moho, the
   core–mantle boundary and the inner core; a reference Earth model.
3. **Lithosphere and Asthenosphere** — `lithosphere-asthenosphere`
   Chemical layers versus mechanical layers; the geotherm; isostasy (Airy
   model) and why continents stand higher than ocean floors.
4. **Plates and Their Motions** — `plate-motions`
   Rigid plates on a sphere; rates from magnetic stripes and from satellite
   positioning; hotspot tracks; the predictive power of the model.
5. **Ocean Ridges and the Oceanic Lithosphere** — `ocean-ridges`
   Basalt, gabbro and peridotite; melting by decompression; hydrothermal
   vents; the lithosphere cools, thickens and sinks as it ages.
6. **Subduction Zones** — `subduction-zones`
   Trenches and dipping earthquake zones; why old lithosphere sinks; the
   slab is transformed at depth (blueschist, eclogite); slab pull.
7. **Minerals** — `minerals`
   Identifying minerals (habit, cleavage, hardness, lustre, density); the
   main rock-forming silicates (olivine, pyroxene, amphibole, mica,
   feldspar, quartz); thin sections in polarised light (photographs).
8. **Igneous Rocks** — `igneous-rocks`
   Volcanic and plutonic textures and the cooling rate; basalt and gabbro,
   andesite and diorite, rhyolite and granite; the order in which minerals
   crystallise (Bowen, told).
9. **The Geology of Coal, Oil and Gas** — `fossil-fuel-geology`
   Preserving organic matter; burial and maturation; source rock,
   reservoir, seal and trap; why rifts and passive margins hold
   hydrocarbons; how coal forms.

### Grade 12
1. **The Continental Crust** — `continental-crust`
   Granite and its relatives; a thick, light and old crust (up to 4 billion
   years); mountain roots and isostasy.
2. **Building Mountains** — `mountain-building`
   Convergence and collision; folds, faults and nappes; ophiolites and
   sutures; the Himalaya and the Alps as examples.
3. **Metamorphic Rocks** — `metamorphic-rocks`
   Pressure and temperature transform rocks; index minerals; foliation and
   schistosity; contact and regional metamorphism.
4. **Magmatism at Subduction Zones** — `subduction-magmatism`
   Water released by the slab melts the mantle above it; andesite and
   granodiorite; how continents grow.
5. **The Destruction of Mountains** — `destruction-of-mountains`
   Weathering, erosion, transport and sedimentation remove relief; isostatic
   rebound; erosion rates; the continental crust recycled.
6. **The Earth's Internal Heat** — `internal-heat`
   Geothermal gradient and heat flow; radioactive heat production (recalled
   from the physics book); conduction and convection; mantle convection;
   geothermal energy.
7. **Radiometric Dating and the Age of the Earth** — `age-of-the-earth`
   The decay law recalled from the physics book; the rubidium–strontium
   isochron; the history of the age of the Earth (cooling, salt, sediment
   rates) up to 4.55 billion years from meteorites.
8. **The Early Earth and Its Atmosphere** — `early-earth`
   The young Earth and its first ocean; the primitive atmosphere; the rise
   of oxygen and its traces in rocks (banded iron formations, red beds).
9. **Reading Past Climates** — `past-climates`
   Air bubbles and isotopes in ice cores; pollen; marine sediments; glacial
   and interglacial cycles; astronomical cycles (told); climates of deep
   time.
10. **Climate Change: Observations, Causes and Projections** — `climate-change`
    Radiative forcing; the observed records; attribution with its confidence
    levels; feedbacks; emission scenarios and projected warming and
    sea-level rise. Physical science only.

## Book 3 — University Year 1 (bachelor-1)

1. **Spherical Astronomy** — `spherical-astronomy`
   Spherical triangles (cosine and sine rules); horizontal and equatorial
   coordinates; sidereal time; rising and setting; the Sun's position and
   insolation; calendars. (Spherical trigonometry is not in the mathematics
   series; this chapter owns it.)
2. **Telescopes, Detectors and Photometry** — `observing-the-sky`
   Atmospheric windows; telescopes and resolution (recalled from the physics
   book); detectors; magnitudes and fluxes; spectrographs; observing from
   space.
3. **Comparative Planetology** — `comparative-planetology`
   Masses, radii, densities and bulk composition; terrestrial planets, gas
   giants and ice giants; atmospheres and surfaces compared; moons and small
   bodies.
4. **Geological Maps** — `geological-maps`
   A map as the intersection of a 3D geometry with topography; strike and
   dip; outcrop patterns (the V-rule, folds, faults, dykes, plutons);
   reading the chronology from a map; digital elevation models and GIS.
5. **Cross-Sections** — `cross-sections`
   Constructing a section from a map; apparent dip; layer thickness;
   boreholes; from section to geological history.
6. **Field and Lab Methods I** — `field-lab-methods-1`
   The field notebook; compass-clinometer; hand lens; sampling; the
   polarising microscope and thin sections; safety. The year's methods
   chapter.
7. **Rock-Forming Minerals** — `rock-forming-minerals`
   Recalls crystal lattices and silicate units from the chemistry book;
   solid solutions (olivine, plagioclase); ionic substitution; mineral
   formulas; the main rock-forming minerals in detail.
8. **Optical Mineralogy** — `optical-mineralogy`
   Polarised light; birefringence and interference colours; extinction;
   identifying minerals in thin section.
9. **The Layered Earth** — `layered-earth`
   Seismic velocities with depth; a reference Earth model; density from
   seismic data; mantle mineralogy with depth (phase transitions); the core.
10. **The Earth's Heat Budget** — `heat-budget`
    Heat sources (radiogenic, secular cooling); heat-flow measurement; the
    steady conductive geotherm with heat production; convection versus
    conduction; the lithosphere as a thermal boundary layer.
11. **Isostasy and Gravity Anomalies** — `isostasy-gravity`
    Airy and Pratt; free-air and Bouguer anomalies; the geoid; the marine
    geoid maps the seafloor.
12. **Plate Kinematics on a Sphere** — `plate-kinematics`
    Euler poles and angular velocities; relative velocities on a sphere;
    triple junctions; the magnetic-anomaly timescale; satellite geodesy.
13. **Rifts, Ridges and Passive Margins** — `rifts-ridges-margins`
    Continental rifting and break-up; passive-margin structure; fast and
    slow ridges; the depth–age law of the ocean floor; transform faults.
14. **Active Margins** — `active-margins`
    Morphology, gravity, heat flow and seismicity of a subduction zone;
    accretionary and erosive margins; slab geometry; volcanic arcs.
15. **Hotspots and Intraplate Volcanism** — `hotspots`
    Mantle plumes; hotspot chains and plate motion; large igneous provinces.
16. **Stress, Strain and Rheology** — `stress-strain-rheology`
    Stress and principal stresses in 2D; strain; pure and simple shear;
    elastic, brittle and ductile behaviour; the strength envelope of the
    lithosphere.
17. **Faults, Folds and Fabrics** — `faults-folds-fabrics`
    Fault types and Anderson's theory; fold geometry; foliation, schistosity
    and lineation; microstructures.
18. **The Earthquake Source** — `earthquake-source`
    Elastic rebound and the seismic cycle; seismic moment and magnitudes;
    focal mechanisms; geodetic measurement of active deformation; the
    Gutenberg–Richter law.
19. **Weathering and Clays** — `weathering-clays`
    Hydrolysis and dissolution; the ionic-potential diagram; clay minerals
    and climate; silicate weathering draws down carbon dioxide.
20. **Erosion and Transport** — `erosion-transport`
    Solute and particle fluxes; the Hjulström diagram; settling velocity;
    denudation rates.
21. **Clastic Sediments and Their Environments** — `clastic-sediments`
    Grain size and sorting; bedforms and sedimentary structures; fluvial,
    deltaic, shoreline and deep-sea deposits.
22. **Carbonate, Siliceous and Evaporite Sediments** — `chemical-biogenic-sediments`
    The carbonate factory; reefs and platforms; pelagic oozes and the
    carbonate compensation depth; siliceous oozes; evaporite sequences.
23. **Diagenesis and Sedimentary Basins** — `diagenesis-basins`
    Compaction and cementation; basin types; subsidence, accommodation and
    sea-level change.
24. **The Stratigraphic Scale** — `stratigraphic-scale`
    Litho-, bio- and chronostratigraphy; boundary stratotypes; mass
    extinctions as global markers (recalled from the biology book); the
    international chart.
25. **Radiometric Dating Methods** — `radiometric-methods`
    Decay systems (K–Ar, Rb–Sr, U–Pb, ¹⁴C); closure temperature; dating
    sedimentary sequences with volcanic ashes; calibrating the scale.
26. **Structure of the Atmosphere and the Ocean** — `fluid-envelopes-structure`
    Lapse rates and potential temperature; static stability; ocean layers
    and T–S diagrams; the density of seawater.
27. **Radiation and Heat Transport** — `radiation-heat-transport`
    Insolation with latitude and season (from spherical astronomy); the
    radiative surplus and deficit; meridional heat transport by the
    atmosphere and the ocean; latent heat.
28. **The General Circulation of the Atmosphere** — `atmospheric-circulation`
    Hadley, Ferrel and polar cells; the intertropical convergence zone;
    Walker cells; jet streams; the geostrophic wind (Coriolis recalled from
    the physics book); depressions and anticyclones.
29. **Ocean Circulation** — `ocean-circulation`
    Wind-driven gyres; Ekman transport and upwelling; western boundary
    currents; the meridional overturning circulation and water masses.

## Book 4 — University Year 2 (bachelor-2)

   Gas and dust between the stars; molecular clouds; the Jeans criterion;
   protostars and their discs.

   differentiation of planets.

4. **Isotope Geochemistry** — `isotope-geochemistry`

   Solidus and geotherm; decompression, flux and heating; degree of

8. **Magmatic Differentiation** — `magmatic-differentiation`
   Fractional crystallisation; compatible and incompatible elements;
   mixing and assimilation.
9. **Magmatic Series and Tectonic Settings** — `magmatic-series`

10. **Metamorphic Reactions and Facies** — `metamorphic-facies`

11. **Thermobarometry and P–T–t Paths** — `pt-paths`

12. **Crustal Melting: Migmatites and Granites** — `crustal-melting`

13. **Anatomy of a Mountain Belt: The Alps** — `alpine-orogen`

14. **Collision and Andean Belts Compared** — `himalaya-andes`
    The Himalaya and Tibet; the Andes; crustal thickening and plateaus.
15. **Ancient Massifs and the Wilson Cycle** — `wilson-cycle`
    Old orogens and what remains of them; the Wilson cycle; supercontinents.
16. **Sedimentary Basins Compared** — `basins-compared`

17. **Oceanic Islands** — `oceanic-islands`
    Growth, flank collapse and erosion of volcanic islands; reefs and atolls;

18. **The Precambrian Earth** — `precambrian-earth`
    Hadean, Archean and Proterozoic; early crust and greenstone belts; the

19. **The Phanerozoic Earth** — `phanerozoic-earth`
    Palaeogeography through time; sea level; orogenies; mass extinctions and

20. **Fossils and Palaeoenvironments** — `palaeoenvironments`

## Conventions for the writing step (confirmed 2026-10-22)

Carried over from the chemistry series unless the user decides otherwise:

- **Exercise calibration** as in chemistry:
  - grades 1–5: 10–11 exercises, mostly ★, no weekend problem;
  - grades 6–9: 12 exercises and a weekend problem of 10–14 questions
    (Parts I–III);
  - grades 10–12: exactly 15 exercises (5★/6★★/4★★★) and a ~20-question
    problem ending on a named number;
  - university years: exactly 12 exercises (4★/5★★/3★★★) and one problem of
    22–28 questions.
- **Sourced data ledger** (shared rows plus one file per book). Every
  measured value (plate rates, heat flow, CO₂ records, sea level, planetary
  data) has a source and an access date. Volatile values (latest CO₂,
  temperature anomaly) go in `dated` boxes.
- **Every curve from a tested `figdata/` script**, real datasets included
  (the Mauna Loa record, ice cores, the USGS earthquake catalogue, sea-level
  records, PREM). No Python is printed as an exercise.
- **Visuals**:
  - exact TikZ for maps, sections, block diagrams, phase and stereographic
    diagrams;
  - photographs (public domain or CC) for real rocks, thin sections,
    landscapes, volcanoes and planets;
  - AI illustrations only for generic scenes, never as a stand-in for a real
    place or body.
- **Boxes**: `recall`, *In the field*, *In the lab*, `history`, `dated`. No
  defined term inside a box.
- **Process**: as chemistry. The user reviews this outline, then a pilot
  chapter or two, then the books.
